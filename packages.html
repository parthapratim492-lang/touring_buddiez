Before You Book

Common questions about
touring Northeast India

Do I need a permit (ILP) to visit Arunachal Pradesh, Sikkim, or Nagaland?

Yes — Indian nationals need an Inner Line Permit (ILP) for Arunachal Pradesh and Nagaland, and a Protected Area Permit for parts of Sikkim near the China border. Foreign nationals need a Protected Area Permit for all three. We handle the permit paperwork as part of every package that requires one, including remote-route trips like Anini, Kaho, and Dong Valley.

What's the best time of year to visit Meghalaya, Sikkim, or Bhutan?

October to April is generally the most reliable window across Northeast India — clear mountain views, drivable roads, and the living root bridges and waterfalls of Meghalaya are easiest to reach. June to September brings the monsoon: Meghalaya gets dramatically greener but some roads and river crossings become unpredictable. Sikkim and Bhutan's high passes are best avoided in peak winter (Dec–Feb) due to snow.

Can I customize a tour package, or are they fixed itineraries?

Every itinerary on this site is a starting point, not a fixed package. We adjust routes, pace, and stops around your actual travel dates, group size, and interests — whether that means adding an extra night somewhere, swapping a stop, or building something from scratch that isn't listed at all.

What vehicles do you provide for car rentals in Guwahati and Northeast India?

Our fleet includes SUVs (Toyota Innova Crysta, Mahindra Scorpio), MPVs (Maruti Ertiga), and sedans (Swift Dzire) — both self-drive and with an experienced local driver. Mountain routes into Meghalaya, Sikkim, and Arunachal Pradesh are typically better suited to an SUV with a driver who knows the roads, especially in monsoon season.

How far in advance should I book a Northeast India tour package?

For peak season (Oct–Dec, and around festivals like Ziro Fest), we'd recommend booking at least 3–4 weeks ahead — permits and remote-route logistics take time to arrange properly. For off-peak travel, 1–2 weeks is usually enough.

Is Northeast India safe for solo and family travelers?

Yes — the routes we run are established tourist and trade routes used daily by local traffic. That said, some areas (border regions, high-altitude passes) genuinely benefit from a local driver-guide rather than self-navigation, which is exactly why we run vehicle-and-driver packages rather than just point-to-point transport.
